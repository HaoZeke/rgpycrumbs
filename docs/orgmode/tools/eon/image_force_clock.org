#+TITLE: Compare image force tangents

The command ~rgpycrumbs eon image-force-clock~ takes prepared path images
and gradients and compares two projections of the inverted potential
gradient. One uses the derivative of the cubic coordinate spline. The
other uses the improved energy-weighted image tangent and one-sided end
chords. Both interpolate the signed force in mass-weighted arc length.

For a model surface, supply a CSV with one column per coordinate, named
gradient columns and an energy column. The optional surface command
receives the Cartesian coordinates and writes an energy as its first
comma-separated field.

#+begin_src bash
rgpycrumbs eon image-force-clock prepared.csv --target-energy -0.145 \
  --surface-command './model surface'
#+end_src

For a molecular calculation, supply the prepared Cartesian image JSON,
the matching energy-gradient JSONL, the seed containing atom masses and
the energy-level record. The largest Cartesian image-to-ledger difference
must be within the specified matching tolerance. The output includes
the observed maximum and the hashes of its inputs.

#+begin_src bash
rgpycrumbs eon image-force-clock images.json --ledger evaluations.jsonl \
  --molecular-seed seed.json --level levels.jsonl
#+end_src

For each projection the program integrates the force spline analytically
in space. It locates the returning root of that primitive and integrates
inverse speed with zero-speed turning endpoints. The half period is
independent of a chosen Verlet step or ring bead count. Distances and
periods use the supplied mass, length and energy units. When a surface
command is supplied, the result includes the measured energy excess at the
return turn, the maximum energy along the path, and the number of extra
surface evaluations.
