#+TITLE: Thermal orbit stationarity
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil todo:nil num:nil author:nil

The ~eon orbit-residual~ command evaluates the Euclidean equation of motion
on the orbit written by an instanton solver. The residual in each mobile
Cartesian coordinate is the physical force plus the mass times the second
imaginary-time derivative of the position. Output includes both the largest
absolute component and the root mean square across components and beads,
in eV/Å.

* Model orbits

For a model with reduced units, ~eon model-orbit-residual~ reads an equal-time Cartesian CSV and takes the full orbit period in those units. Supply one mass for each named coordinate. The surface program receives the coordinates as positional arguments and writes one CSV row containing the energy followed by the Cartesian potential gradient. The command evaluates each bead separately and reports both finite-bead and Fourier force residuals, including the maximum component, its bead index, and the RMS over all components and beads.

For an orbit with columns ~x~ and ~y~, unit masses, and a measured half-period of 8.143223, the command is

#+begin_src sh
rgpycrumbs eon model-orbit-residual ring.csv --coordinate x --coordinate y \
  --mass 1 --mass 1 --period 16.286446 --surface-command './lineb_muller_brown surface'
#+end_src

* Inputs

Supply a readcon band containing a complete thermal period, with one
~tau_over_beta~ fraction in each frame. Place the beads at uniform intervals
and omit a repeated endpoint. Use continuous atomic coordinates across the
period. The command reads masses, atom IDs, fixed masks and cell geometry
from the band. If frames do not specify ~temperature_K~, supply
~--temperature~ in kelvin.

The independent force provider is a factory named by ~module:function~ or
~/path/to/file.py:function~. It receives the band path and keyword arguments
from ~--options~. Its ~cartesian_forces(frame)~ method returns an array of
shape ~(atoms, 3)~ in eV/Å. ~result_counts()~ is optional and returns
cumulative ~computed~ and ~served~ geometry counts. The report records the
work of the independent check under ~evaluation_counts.scope = validation~.

#+begin_src bash
rgpycrumbs eon orbit-residual ring.con \
    --provider potential.py:orbit_provider --temperature 150
#+end_src

A stationary saddle repeated at every bead can have a small force residual.
The ~geometry_extent~ record gives the number of distinct coordinate arrays,
the largest single-atom displacement between any two beads in Å, and the
largest displacement in mass-weighted Cartesian space in Å √u. All three use
the coordinates as stored. Independent rotations or translations of beads
contribute to the measured span. To track an internal coordinate, add
~--atom-pair 2 5~ with the two atom IDs from the band. The ~pair_distances~
record reports their minimum and maximum separation in Å over the period.

The calculation differentiates the periodic Fourier interpolant on the bead
grid. Repeating the check at higher bead resolution distinguishes a
discretization residual from an error that persists in the path.

An eOn ~lineb-orbit.con~ carries the potential gradient for every mobile atom
in each rate bead. Omit the force provider to read these measurements, convert
the gradient to a force and use twice the recorded half-period. The report
gives the finite-bead force residual, its cost of zero additional potential
calls and the number of observed beads. ~--compare-discretizations~ adds the
Fourier residual from the same measured gradients. A separate force provider
uses the thermal period from the physical constants and the reported temperature.

#+begin_src sh
rgpycrumbs eon orbit-residual lineb-orbit.con --compare-discretizations
#+end_src

* Tangential and normal forces

For a full readcon period carrying measured gradients, run

#+begin_src sh
rgpycrumbs eon orbit-residual lineb-orbit.con --decompose-residual
#+end_src

At each moving bead the command differentiates the periodic Cartesian Fourier
interpolant and normalizes the velocity in mass-weighted coordinates. It
projects the Fourier residual divided by the square root of the masses onto
this tangent and its orthogonal complement. Multiplication by the square root
of the masses returns both projections to Cartesian force units. Their maxima
and RMS values use the same moving beads. The normal fraction is the sum of
squared normal components divided by the total, both in the inverse mass metric.

The ~zero_velocity_beads~ list identifies samples where the tangent is
undefined to Fourier roundoff, including turning points sampled exactly.
The original residual still includes every bead. The projection cites the
SHA-256 of the input orbit. With recorded gradients, it uses zero additional
surface evaluations.
